PG&E Corporation

PCGUtilities

PG&E fell 20.1% after California lawmakers advanced a narrower wildfire bill that did not deliver the durable liability and financing protections utilities sought. The lower entry price and improving earnings profile are interesting, but high leverage, negative free cash flow, and open-ended wildfire exposure prevent a survivable bear case from being bounded responsibly.

What would upgrade it: Enacted legislation or binding regulatory treatment that clearly caps incremental wildfire exposure, together with evidence that PG&E can fund its capital plan without unsupported dilution, would justify fresh valuation and return underwriting.

Edison International

EIXUtilities

Edison International fell 23.1% as the California compromise left insurer claims and core utility wildfire liability unresolved. The valuation now looks optically low, but Southern California Edison's existing wildfire claims, rising debt, and persistently negative free cash flow make the apparent discount inseparable from a potentially severe downside tail.

What would upgrade it: A clearer estimate of net wildfire liabilities, confirmed access to the state fund, and a durable legislative framework that preserves investment-grade financing without large equity issuance would permit a responsible expected-return range.

NIO Inc.

NIOConsumer Cyclical

NIO delivered 35,836 vehicles in August, up 14.5% year over year, and year-to-date deliveries rose 57.9%. That confirms scale growth, but August volume slipped slightly from July and the fixed cutoff precedes the same-day second-quarter report needed to verify margins, cash generation, liquidity, and dilution risk.

What would upgrade it: Cutoff-safe financial results showing durable positive operating cash flow, improving gross margin across all three brands, and no reliance on material shareholder dilution would support full quality, downside, and expected-return underwriting.
